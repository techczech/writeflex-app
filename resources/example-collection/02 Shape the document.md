# Shape the document

Every line is a block, and a heading carries everything under it. This file is a short essay to rearrange. Where a command has no key, its name is in bold: press <kbd>⇧⌘P</kbd>, type the name and press <kbd>↵</kbd>.

> **Fold.** Put the caret on the heading **Leaving the village** and press <kbd>⌘8</kbd> to fold it. <kbd>⌘9</kbd> folds every heading; <kbd>⌃⌘0</kbd> unfolds them all.

## Leaving the village

The first half mile is all hedgerow. In September the hawthorn is heavy with haws and the blackberries have gone soft and dark.

You hear the road for longer than you expect, and then, somewhere past the second stile, you stop hearing it at all.

> **Move blocks and sections.** Put the caret on the heading **The wall builders** and press <kbd>⇧⌥⌘↑</kbd> to move it, with everything under it, above **Up on the wold**. <kbd>⇧⌥⌘↓</kbd> moves it back. On a single line, <kbd>⌥⌘↑</kbd> and <kbd>⌥⌘↓</kbd> move just that block. <kbd>⌃⌘A</kbd> selects this section; <kbd>⌃⌘⌫</kbd> deletes it, and <kbd>⌘Z</kbd> brings it back.

## Up on the wold

At the top the land opens out. The fields are large and pale, the walls are dry stone, and the sky takes up most of the view.

## The wall builders

Every wall up here was built by hand, stone by stone, without mortar. A good waller can lay a few metres in a day.

> **Headings and levels.** **The wall builders** belongs under **Up on the wold**. With the caret on it, press <kbd>⌥⌘→</kbd> to lower its level and <kbd>⌥⌘←</kbd> to raise it again. <kbd>⇧⌥⌘→</kbd> and <kbd>⇧⌥⌘←</kbd> do the same with everything under it. On the line **Weather** below, <kbd>⌃⌘M</kbd> makes it a heading at the surrounding level and <kbd>⌃⇧⌘M</kbd> one level deeper.

Weather

The weather arrives from the west, and on the wold you see it an hour before it reaches you.

> **New headings.** <kbd>⌃⌘1</kbd>, <kbd>⌃⌘2</kbd>, <kbd>⌃⌘3</kbd>, <kbd>⌃⌘4</kbd>, <kbd>⌃⌘5</kbd> and <kbd>⌃⌘6</kbd> insert a heading of that level. With the caret on the heading **Coming down**, press <kbd>⌘K</kbd> and choose **New heading before this** or **New subheading under this**.

## Coming down

The path drops through a hanging wood, and the village roofs come back one at a time.

> **Jump between headings.** Press <kbd>⌃⌘]</kbd> for the next heading and <kbd>⌃⌘[</kbd> for the previous one. <kbd>⌃⌘↓</kbd> and <kbd>⌃⌘↑</kbd> step one block at a time. <kbd>⇧⌘O</kbd> opens the Outline, which lists every heading in this file.

## What the long way teaches

It takes twice as long, and I remember four times as much of it.

> **The Outline.** These commands act on the Outline, so open it first. **Outline: Collapse all** and **Outline: Expand all** close and open every heading in the list. **Outline: Show levels 1 to 2** lists headings down to the second level only, and there is one such command for each level from 1 to 6; **Outline: Show all levels** lists them all again. **Outline: Show / hide word counts** puts the number of words beside each heading. **Outline: Open the menu for this row** opens the menu for the heading you have selected in the list.
