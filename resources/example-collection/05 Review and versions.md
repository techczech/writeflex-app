# Review and versions

There is nothing to save: the file is written to disk as you type. This file is a first draft of the part about the wall builders, with sentences that need work. Use it to try checkpoints, versions and review. Where a command has no key, its name is in bold: press <kbd>⇧⌘P</kbd>, type the name and press <kbd>↵</kbd>.

> **Drafting and Reviewing.** The button at the top right says Drafting or Reviewing, and <kbd>⌥⌘M</kbd> switches between them. Your edits are recorded in both. Drafting hides the marks; Reviewing shows them, with what was taken out struck through. Switch to Reviewing, then delete **any** and **at all** from the first sentence under **The wall builders**.

## The wall builders

Every wall up here was built by hand, stone by stone, without any mortar at all. A good waller can lay a few metres in a day, which is not very much when you think about it.

The stone comes out of the field the wall goes round. Nobody carried it far. The big stones go at the bottom and the wall narrows as it rises, and every yard or so a long stone is laid right through from one face to the other to tie the two sides together.

> **Review sessions.** A review session is one sitting of changes. **Start a new review session** begins one, so that what you change from now on is listed apart from what came before. <kbd>⇧⌘C</kbd> opens Review in the right-hand pane, which lists the changes in this file. **Accept session** accepts every change in the session at once.

> **Review changes.** Make two or three more changes to the paragraphs above. <kbd>⌥⌘G</kbd> goes to the next change and <kbd>⇧⌥⌘G</kbd> to the previous one. <kbd>⌥⌘↵</kbd> accepts the change you are on and moves to the next. Select a change you have already accepted in Review: **Return change to review** makes it open again, and **Send original to Scratchpad** keeps the words it replaced as a scrap. **Hide strikes** hides what was taken out, and **Hide inserts** hides the marking of what was put in; choosing either again shows them.

## The gaps

A wall that is left alone lasts a hundred years. What brings it down is a tree root, or a ewe that has learned where it is loose, or a walker who climbs it instead of going on to the stile.

When a length falls it is called a gap, and mending one is called gapping. The waller takes the fallen stone back out to the two sound ends, sorts it by size on the grass, and builds it up again in the same order.

> **Versions and compare.** Press <kbd>⌘S</kbd> to make a checkpoint: a state of the file you can go back to. Closing the file makes one too. <kbd>⇧⌘S</kbd> saves a version with a name, such as First draft. <kbd>⌘3</kbd> opens Versions in the right-hand pane, which lists both kinds. <kbd>⌥⌘C</kbd> compares versions side by side, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> returns to the file.
