# Review and versions

There is nothing to save: the file is written to disk as you type. This file is a first draft of the part about the wall builders, with sentences that need work. Use it to try checkpoints, versions and review. Where a command has no key, its name is in bold: press <kbd>⇧⌘P</kbd>, type the name and press <kbd>↵</kbd>.

> **Drafting and Reviewing.** The button at the top right says Drafting or Reviewing, and <kbd>⌥⌘M</kbd> switches between them. Your edits are recorded in both. Drafting hides the marks; Reviewing shows them, with what was taken out struck through. Switch to Reviewing, then delete **any** and **at all** from the first sentence under **The wall builders**.

## The wall builders

Every wall up here was built by hand, stone by stone, without any mortar at all. A good waller can lay a few metres in a day, which is not very much when you think about it.

The stone comes out of the field the wall goes round. Nobody carried it far. The big stones go at the bottom and the wall narrows as it rises, and every yard or so a long stone is laid right through from one face to the other to tie the two sides together.

> **Review sessions.** A review session is one sitting of changes. **Start a new review session** begins one, so that what you change from now on is listed apart from what came before. The margin beside the text shows the comments and edits in this file as cards. **Accept session** accepts every change in the session at once.

> **Review changes.** Make two or three more changes to the paragraphs above. They appear in the margin as comments and edits.
>
> Stepping through them: <kbd>⌥⌘G</kbd> goes to the next item and <kbd>⇧⌥⌘G</kbd> to the previous one, and puts you in its card. There <kbd>A</kbd> accepts it, <kbd>D</kbd> declines it and <kbd>R</kbd> replies; Esc goes back to the text. <kbd>⌥⌘↵</kbd> accepts the item you are on and moves to the next. <kbd>⇧⌘C</kbd> goes to the first item shown. When items are waiting in other pieces, the margin says how many, and <kbd>⌃⌥⌘G</kbd> opens the next piece with items waiting.
>
> Where the cards sit: <kbd>⇧⌥⌘V</kbd> switches the cards between Margin and Inline, which puts them under their paragraphs. Below about 1150 pixels of window width they go Inline on their own. <kbd>⇧⌥⌘M</kbd> shows or hides the margin. When several items sit on one paragraph they become one stack of one-line rows, and nothing folds or opens while you are typing in it.
>
> Filtering and bulk actions are in the review line in the status bar and its menu. The line counts what is waiting. <kbd>⌃⌥⌘1</kbd> shows all, <kbd>⌃⌥⌘2</kbd> shows comments only and <kbd>⌃⌥⌘3</kbd> shows edits only, and the next and previous keys then step through what is shown. <kbd>⇧⌘A</kbd> accepts everything shown, or only the cards you have selected with Command-click. <kbd>⇧⌘⌫</kbd> declines them, after asking you first, and <kbd>⇧⌘R</kbd> sends the review back to the agent. **Accept all shown, or the selected**, **Decline all shown, or the selected** and **Send the review back to the agent** do the same from the palette. Changes from one agent outside WriteFlex, such as Claude Code or Codex, can be settled across the whole collection at once: **Accept all from one agent…** and **Decline all from one agent…** in the palette, or the same rows under "From outside, across this collection" in the review line's menu. Each asks first and says how many changes in how many documents; Accept all accepts each change as a click on Accept would, and leaves any it cannot place waiting.
>
> Three more views are modes in the palette, and they are there even when nothing is waiting; Esc goes back to review. **Settled today** shows what you accepted or declined today beside its words; Reopen on its card makes one wait again. **My edits** shows your own recorded edits as cards, with Accept all for the session. **Show quiet fixes** shows the small fixes that otherwise wait without a card.
>
> Select a change you have already accepted: **Return change to review** makes it open again, and **Send original to Scratchpad** keeps the words it replaced as a scrap. **Hide strikes** hides what was taken out, and **Hide inserts** hides the marking of what was put in; choosing either again shows them.

## The gaps

A wall that is left alone lasts a hundred years. What brings it down is a tree root, or a ewe that has learned where it is loose, or a walker who climbs it instead of going on to the stile.

When a length falls it is called a gap, and mending one is called gapping. The waller takes the fallen stone back out to the two sound ends, sorts it by size on the grass, and builds it up again in the same order.

Closing the file makes one too. <kbd>⇧⌘S</kbd> saves a version with a name, such as First draft. <kbd>⌘3</kbd> opens Versions in the right-hand pane, which lists both kinds. <kbd>⌥⌘C</kbd> compares versions side by side, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> returns to the file.
